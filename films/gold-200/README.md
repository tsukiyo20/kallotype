# KODAK GOLD 200

The consumer negative: three panels instead of four, no MTF anywhere in the
sheet, and a colour curve whose unconstrained top once gave its own base density
a negative value — the clearest thing the rising fit ever fixed.

- **The report:** [REPORT.md](REPORT.md) — the base density that was −0.042, the
  printed anchor, the D-min mask that is the densest in the family, and the two
  quantities the sheet cannot supply.
- **The film:** `gold-200.kalofilm` (6,237 bytes) — load it with
  `kalo render --film-file`.
- **The points:** `curves/` — 128 points per layer.
- **The figures:** `figures/` — `01-curves`, `02-residuals`, `03-spectra`, drawn
  from `curves/` and the file's own tables.

Headline numbers: the curves climb to 1.59 (red), 1.68 (green) and 1.77 (blue)
above their bases; base density 0.255, 0.644 and 0.984 — the densest of the Kodak
negatives; γ 0.552, 0.593 and 0.619 per decade; residuals 0.0059, 0.0060 and
0.0095; layer sensitivities peak at 650, 550 and 470 nm and are the flattest in
the family (within 28 per cent); the mask runs from 0.996 of a density at 440 nm
to 0.238 at 700. At the sheet's printed reference exposure the green record sits
0.745 above its base, against the 0.70 a C-41 negative is balanced on. Grain is
not claimed — the sheet publishes a Print Grain Index that cannot be compared to
rms granularity — and neither is blur: the sheet has no modulation transfer
function at all. The dye shapes are borrowed from the VISION3 500T digitisation.
